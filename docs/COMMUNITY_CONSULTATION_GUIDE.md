# Community Consultation Guide
## PreRecoveryPath South Africa

**Purpose:** Ensure the platform reflects and respects the diverse needs, values, and cultural contexts of South African communities affected by substance abuse.

---

## 1. Core Principles

### 1.1 Why Community Consultation Matters

**"Nothing About Us Without Us"**

We do not build a platform FOR communities—we build it WITH communities. Those affected by substance abuse are experts in their own experiences and must guide platform development.

**Goals:**
- Understand diverse cultural approaches to recovery
- Identify community resources and support systems
- Learn what works and what doesn't in each context
- Build trust and local ownership
- Avoid cultural imposition or harm

### 1.2 Consultation Ethics

**Respect:**
- Value community knowledge equally with academic expertise
- Compensate participants fairly for their time
- Honor cultural protocols and sensitivities

**Transparency:**
- Clear explanation of how input will be used
- No false promises about implementation
- Share results back with communities

**Inclusivity:**
- Reach marginalized voices, not just accessible ones
- Accommodate language, literacy, and access barriers
- Include people at various recovery stages

## 2. Nine-Province Listening Tour

### 2.1 Tour Objectives

**For Each Province:**
1. Understand local substance abuse patterns and challenges
2. Identify existing community support systems
3. Learn cultural approaches to healing and recovery
4. Map resources (formal and informal)
5. Build relationships with local stakeholders
6. Identify potential pilot site partners

### 2.2 Provincial Tour Structure

**Each Province Visit (5 days):**

**Day 1: Stakeholder Orientation**
- Meet with provincial health department
- Visit SANCA branch
- Connect with local NGOs
- Map community resources

**Day 2: Community Leader Consultation**
- Traditional leaders and healers
- Faith leaders
- Community health workers
- Municipal officials

**Day 3: Focus Groups - People Affected**
- People in recovery
- Family members
- Youth at risk
- Elderly community members

**Day 4: Service Provider Consultation**
- Addiction counselors
- Social workers
- Healthcare providers
- Peer counselors

**Day 5: Synthesis and Feedback**
- Share initial insights with community
- Refine understanding
- Identify pilot partners
- Plan follow-up

### 2.3 Province-Specific Considerations

#### Gauteng (Urban, Diverse)
- **Focus:** Urban drug market dynamics, nyaope epidemic, refugee/migrant populations
- **Languages:** All 11 languages present, multilingual approach essential
- **Cultural:** High diversity, formal services more available
- **Venues:** Community halls, clinics, churches, shelters

#### Western Cape (Cape Town, Rural)
- **Focus:** Tik (methamphetamine), gang involvement, colored communities
- **Languages:** Afrikaans, English, isiXhosa
- **Cultural:** Distinct colored identity, gang culture, rural Afrikaans communities
- **Venues:** Community centers, churches, NA meetings, rural towns

#### KwaZulu-Natal (Zulu, Traditional)
- **Focus:** Alcohol abuse, traditional healing integration, rural access
- **Languages:** isiZulu, English
- **Cultural:** Strong traditional structures, elder respect, collective healing
- **Venues:** Traditional council spaces, churches, SANCA, homesteads

#### Eastern Cape (Rural, Xhosa)
- **Focus:** Alcohol, poverty-related substance use, rural isolation
- **Languages:** isiXhosa, English
- **Cultural:** Xhosa traditions, initiation practices, rural poverty
- **Venues:** Rural health centers, traditional healers, churches

#### Limpopo (Rural, Traditional)
- **Focus:** Traditional healing central, multiple ethnic groups, rural challenges
- **Languages:** Sepedi, Xitsonga, Tshivenda, English
- **Cultural:** Strong traditional beliefs, multiple cultural groups, rural
- **Venues:** Traditional healer associations, clinics, community gatherings

#### Mpumalanga (Mixed)
- **Focus:** Mining communities, cross-border drug trade, siSwati culture
- **Languages:** siSwati, isiZulu, Sepedi, English
- **Cultural:** Mining culture, migrant labor patterns
- **Venues:** Mine compounds, community centers, churches

#### North West (Tswana, Mining)
- **Focus:** Mining communities, platinum belt, rural Tswana areas
- **Languages:** Setswana, English
- **Cultural:** Tswana traditions, mining impact on communities
- **Venues:** Mining areas, traditional kgotla, churches

#### Free State (Rural, Farming)
- **Focus:** Farm communities, unemployment, Sesotho culture
- **Languages:** Sesotho, Afrikaans, English
- **Cultural:** Farming communities, Basotho traditions, rural isolation
- **Venues:** Farm areas, churches, traditional councils

#### Northern Cape (Sparse, Diverse)
- **Focus:** Geographic isolation, San/Khoikhoi communities, mining towns
- **Languages:** Afrikaans, Setswana, English, San languages
- **Cultural:** Sparse population, indigenous San culture, desert communities
- **Venues:** Small towns, mining communities, remote areas (extended visits)

## 3. Focus Group Methodology

### 3.1 Participant Recruitment

**Recruitment Channels:**
- SANCA branches
- Local NGOs
- Traditional healer networks
- Faith communities
- Community health workers
- Word of mouth through local contacts

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- 18+ years old (separate youth protocol)
- South Africa resident
- Experience with substance abuse (personal or family)
- Willing to share experiences
- Informed consent provided

**Diversity Goals:**
- Gender balance
- Age diversity (young adults, middle-aged, elderly)
- Urban and rural representation
- Various substances and recovery stages
- Cultural and linguistic diversity

### 3.2 Focus Group Structure

**Size:** 6-10 participants per group

**Duration:** 90-120 minutes

**Setting:**
- Comfortable, private space
- Culturally appropriate venue
- Accessible location
- Refreshments provided

**Compensation:**
- R200 per participant
- Transport reimbursement
- Childcare if needed
- Meal provided

### 3.3 Focus Group Topics

**Session 1: Current Challenges**
- What makes recovery difficult in your community?
- What support exists? What's missing?
- How do you currently seek help?
- Barriers to accessing support
- Role of stigma

**Session 2: Cultural Context**
- How does your culture view substance abuse?
- Traditional healing approaches that work
- Role of family and community
- Spiritual/faith dimensions
- Communication preferences

**Session 3: Technology and Support**
- Current technology use (phones, data access)
- Experience with digital health tools
- Privacy concerns
- Preferences for digital vs. in-person support
- What would be helpful? What would be harmful?

**Session 4: Platform Concept Feedback**
- React to platform concept
- Concerns and hopes
- Feature suggestions
- Cultural adaptation needs
- Red flags or ethical concerns

### 3.4 Facilitation Approach

**Facilitator Qualities:**
- Fluent in local language(s)
- Cultural knowledge and sensitivity
- Trained in qualitative research methods
- Understands addiction and recovery
- Non-judgmental, empathetic

**Facilitation Principles:**
- Create safe, non-judgmental space
- Honor all contributions equally
- Probe for depth without pushing
- Watch for signs of distress
- Respect confidentiality
- Use culturally appropriate communication styles

**Ethical Safeguards:**
- Option to skip questions or leave anytime
- No pressure to share specific details
- Counselor available if needed
- Check-in after session
- Clear boundaries about confidentiality

## 4. Key Stakeholder Consultations

**Consultation Goals:**
- Clinical best practices for substance abuse treatment in SA
- Risk assessment for digital platform
- Integration with formal treatment
- Crisis intervention protocols
- Vulnerable population considerations

**Questions:**
- What works in SA addiction treatment?
- What are biggest barriers?
- How could technology help? How could it harm?
- Red flags for platform design?
- Collaboration opportunities?

### 4.2 Traditional Healers

**Consultation Approach:**
- Respectful protocol (may require gifts, proper introductions)
- Honor traditional knowledge systems
- Acknowledge healing authority
- Explore collaboration, not extraction

**Discussion Topics:**
- Traditional understanding of substance problems
- Traditional healing approaches
- Role of ancestors and spirituality
- How Western and traditional approaches can work together
- Concerns about technology in healing
- Appropriate integration methods

**Cultural Protocols:**
- Proper introduction through respected community members
- May require consultation fees or gifts
- Respect for sacred knowledge (some may not be shareable)
- Recognition of healing authority
- Follow-up and ongoing relationship

### 4.3 Faith Leaders

**Consultation Goals:**

**Faith Traditions to Include:**

## 5. Cultural Sensitivity Protocols

### 5.1 Language

**Primary Principle:** Always offer consultation in participant's home language

**Implementation:**
- Hire local language speakers as facilitators
- Translate all materials in advance
- Provide interpretation if needed
- Written materials in relevant languages
- Audio options for low literacy

### 5.2 Cultural Protocols

**Respect for Elders:**
- Acknowledge elder wisdom and authority
- Appropriate greetings and respect (e.g., Sawubona in isiZulu)
- Defer to elders in group settings
- Seek elder blessing for community work

**Collectivism vs. Individualism:**
- Frame recovery in community context, not just individual
- Recognize family and community responsibility
- Group-oriented approaches may be more appropriate
- Privacy concerns may differ from Western assumptions

**Gender Considerations:**
- Some topics may need gender-separated groups
- Respect for gender roles and norms
- Address gendered dimensions of substance abuse
- Include women's voices (often marginalized)

**Traditional Authority:**
- Recognize traditional leaders and structures
- Seek permission to work in communities
- Follow customary protocols
- Don't undermine traditional authority

### 5.3 Power Dynamics

**Addressing Power Imbalances:**
- Acknowledge we are learners, communities are teachers
- Share decision-making power
- Transparent about what we can and cannot promise
- Compensation signals value for time and knowledge
- Follow-up and accountability

**Avoiding Extraction:**
- Not just taking information and leaving
- Build ongoing relationships
- Share results and benefits
- Community ownership and governance
- Employment of community members

## 6. Low/No Digital Access Accommodations

### 6.1 Reaching Digitally Excluded

**Challenges:**
- No smartphone access
- No data or limited data
- Low digital literacy
- Remote geographic areas

**Accommodations:**
- In-person consultations in accessible locations
- Paper-based feedback forms
- Voice recordings (if literate barriers)
- Community meetings (group format)
- Partner with community health workers
- Extended time in remote areas

### 6.2 Accessibility Features

**Physical Accessibility:**
- Wheelchair accessible venues
- Transport provided or reimbursed
- Venues accessible by public transport
- Home visits for those unable to travel

**Literacy Accommodations:**
- Oral discussions (not requiring reading/writing)
- Visual materials
- Demonstration and discussion
- No stigma around literacy levels

**Time Accommodations:**
- Flexible scheduling (evenings, weekends)
- Childcare support
- Eldercare support
- Meal provisions for longer sessions

## 7. Data Collection and Analysis

### 7.1 Data Collection Methods

**Qualitative Data:**
- Audio recordings (with permission)
- Facilitator notes
- Participant observation
- Visual mapping exercises
- Storytelling

**Quantitative Data:**
- Demographic information
- Resource mapping
- Priority ranking exercises
- Feedback surveys

### 7.2 Ethical Data Practices

**Consent:**
- Informed consent for recording and data use
- Option to participate without recording
- Anonymity in reporting
- Right to withdraw data

**Storage:**
- Secure storage of recordings and notes
- Anonymization of transcripts
- Limited access to identifiable data
- Deletion after analysis (per agreement)

**POPIA Compliance:**
- Minimal data collection
- Clear purpose stated
- Time-limited retention
- Participant data rights respected

### 7.3 Analysis Approach

**Thematic Analysis:**
- Code transcripts for recurring themes
- Look for patterns within and across provinces
- Identify cultural variations
- Note contradictions and complexity

**Community Validation:**
- Share preliminary findings with communities
- Confirm interpretation accuracy
- Refine analysis based on feedback
- Co-create recommendations

## 9. Timeline

### 9.1 Preparation Phase (2 months)

**Month 1:**
- Develop consultation materials
- Recruit local facilitators and translators
- Build stakeholder relationships
- Plan logistics

**Month 2:**
- Finalize protocols
- Train facilitators
- Confirm venues and participants
- Begin stakeholder mapping

### 9.2 Consultation Phase (5 months)

**Months 3-7:**
- Conduct nine-province listening tour
- One province per 2-3 weeks
- Ongoing data collection and preliminary analysis

### 9.3 Analysis and Reporting (2 months)

**Month 8:**
- Transcription and analysis
- Theme identification
- Community validation sessions

**Month 9:**
- Report writing
- Community report translation
- Presentation preparation

### 9.4 Dissemination and Integration (Ongoing)

**Month 10+:**
- Share reports with communities
- Present findings to stakeholders
- Integrate into platform design
- Ongoing community engagement
