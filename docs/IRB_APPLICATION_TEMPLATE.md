## 1. Research Summary

### 1.1 Background and Rationale

Substance abuse is a major public health challenge in South Africa, with limited accessible support systems, particularly in underserved communities. While digital health interventions show promise, there are significant ethical risks when using AI and algorithms with vulnerable populations, including:

- Platform addiction (using addictive algorithms to create new dependencies)
- Privacy violations (collecting unnecessary sensitive data)
- Cultural imposition (Western-centric approaches)
- Manipulation (prioritizing engagement over genuine support)

This study evaluates a novel ethical AI platform designed specifically to avoid these harms while supporting substance abuse prevention and recovery in South Africa.

### 1.2 Research Questions

**Primary:**
1. Can an ethical AI platform support substance abuse recovery without creating platform dependency?
2. Do real-world outcomes (substance-free days, quality of life) improve for users?
3. Is platform usage decreasing over time as users build independence (as intended)?

**Secondary:**
1. Are the anti-addiction safeguards effective?
2. Is the platform culturally appropriate across SA's diverse populations?
3. Do users maintain autonomy and control throughout their engagement?
4. Are vulnerable populations adequately protected?

### 1.3 Study Design

**Type:** Mixed-methods pilot study with community-based participatory research approach

**Sample Size:** 200 participants across 3 provinces (Gauteng, Western Cape, KwaZulu-Natal)

**Duration:** 
- 6-month active intervention
- 6-month follow-up (minimal platform access)

**Setting:** Community-based recruitment through SANCA, AA/NA, and community health centers

## 2. Ethical Considerations

### 2.1 Novel Ethical Risks: Platform Dependency

**Risk Description:**  
Unlike traditional research risks, this study involves a platform that COULD be designed to be addictive (like TikTok, Instagram). While our platform explicitly avoids addictive design patterns, the risk of creating a new dependency in vulnerable populations must be carefully monitored.

**Risk Mitigation:**
- **Ethics Circuit Breaker:** Automated system blocks manipulative patterns
- **Usage Monitoring:** Weekly audits flag excessive use (>30 min/day, >5 sessions/day)
- **Independent Ethics Board:** 28-member board with veto power reviews all platform features
- **Decreasing Usage as Success:** We measure success by users needing the platform LESS, not MORE
- **Human-First Design:** AI is supplementary; after 3 AI sessions, human connection required
- **Mandatory Session Limits:** 10-minute warnings, 30-minute daily maximum enforced

**Monitoring Plan:**
- Weekly usage pattern review by study team
- Monthly ethics board review during pilot
- Red flags trigger immediate participant check-in
- Option to exit study anytime without penalty

### 2.2 Vulnerable Population Protections

**Vulnerable Populations in Study:**
- People in early recovery (<90 days): High risk of relapse, seeking support
- Youth (18-25): Developmental vulnerability, digital native risks
- Low socioeconomic status: Resource constraints, potential exploitation
- Low literacy: Comprehension and autonomy challenges

**Additional Protections:**
- **Early Recovery:** Human counselor check-ins required, AI not primary support, lower crisis thresholds
- **Youth:** Parental notification option, no gamification, screen time warnings
- **Low SES:** Free data bundles, no cost to participate, accessible locations
- **Low Literacy:** Oral consent option, plain language (8th grade level), visual aids

**Enhanced Consent Process:**
- Extended consent discussions (not just form signing)
- Teach-back method to confirm understanding
- Ongoing consent (can withdraw anytime)
- Advocate present if desired

### 2.3 Privacy and Confidentiality (POPIA Compliance)

**Data Minimization:**
- Collect ONLY data essential for support services
- No exact GPS locations (municipality level only)
- No social graphs without explicit consent
- No background monitoring

**Data Security:**
- Encrypted storage on South African servers
- Access limited to research team
- Anonymized data for analysis
- No sharing with third parties without consent

**Participant Rights:**
- View all collected data anytime
- Export data in readable format
- Delete data and withdraw from study
- Modify or correct data

**POPIA Compliance:**
- Information officer designated
- Data subject rights respected (access, rectification, erasure, portability)
- 30-day response time for data requests
- Breach notification within 24 hours

### 2.4 Cultural Sensitivity and Inclusivity

**Cultural Risks:**
- Imposing Western recovery models
- Disrespecting traditional healing
- Excluding non-English speakers
- Urban-centric assumptions

**Mitigations:**
- 11 official SA languages supported
- Traditional healer consultation and integration
- Provincial cultural adaptation
- Community advisory boards guide implementation
- Local success stories and role models

**Community Involvement:**
- Community members on research team
- Local advisory boards in each province
- Results shared with communities
- Community validation of findings

## 3. Study Methods

### 3.1 Participant Recruitment

**Recruitment Sites:**
- SANCA branches (Gauteng, Western Cape, KZN)
- AA/NA meetings (with permission)
- Community health centers
- NGO partners

**Recruitment Method:**
- Flyers and posters at recruitment sites
- Information sessions
- Peer referral (with participant permission)
- Community health worker outreach

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- 18+ years old
- South Africa resident
- Seeking substance abuse support or in recovery
- Access to smartphone or willing to use at community center
- Able to provide informed consent
- Fluent in at least one of the 11 SA languages

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Active psychosis or severe mental illness requiring immediate treatment
- Immediate crisis requiring emergency services (referred to appropriate care)
- Unable to provide informed consent
- Currently enrolled in another substance abuse intervention study

**Sample Composition Goals:**
- Gender balance (50% women)
- Age diversity (18-25, 26-45, 46-65, 65+)
- Provincial representation
- Language diversity
- Various substances (alcohol, drugs, multiple)
- Recovery stages (early, established, maintaining)

### 3.2 Informed Consent Process

**Consent Materials:**
- Written consent form (8th-grade reading level)
- Available in all 11 SA languages
- Visual aids explaining platform and study
- Oral consent option for low literacy
- Video explanation available

**Consent Discussion:**
- Private, comfortable setting
- Extended time for questions (minimum 30 minutes)
- Teach-back method: participant explains study back to researcher
- Emphasis on voluntary participation and right to withdraw
- Disclosure of all risks, including platform dependency risk
- Explanation of data practices and privacy rights

**Ongoing Consent:**
- Re-consent at 3-month midpoint
- Regular reminders of right to withdraw
- Any protocol changes trigger re-consent

**Documentation:**
- Signed consent form (or documented oral consent)
- Comprehension assessment notes
- Copy provided to participant
- Contact information for questions

### 3.3 Intervention

**Platform Access:**
- 6 months of full platform access
- Features include:
  - Personalized support resources
  - Crisis detection and human escalation
  - Connection to local support services
  - Cultural and language-appropriate content
  - AI companion (supplementary only, with limits)

**Usage Requirements:**
- No minimum usage required (autonomy preserved)
- Maximum 30 minutes per day enforced
- After 3 AI sessions, human connection required
- Weekly autonomy audits
- Session limits (10-minute warnings)

**Human Support Integration:**
- Participants encouraged to continue existing support (SANCA, AA/NA, counseling)
- Platform facilitates connection to human services
- Study does NOT replace existing treatment
- Counselor available if concerns arise

### 3.4 Data Collection

**Baseline (Week 0):**
- Demographics
- Substance use history
- Current support systems
- Quality of life assessment
- Mental health screening
- Digital literacy assessment
- Cultural background and preferences

**During Intervention (Months 1-6):**
- Platform usage data (automated):
  - Daily minutes, session count, time of day
  - Feature usage patterns
  - Crisis events triggered
- Weekly brief surveys (5 minutes):
  - Substance use past week
  - Support sought (human and AI)
  - Perceived helpfulness
- Monthly assessments (20 minutes):
  - Quality of life
  - Social support
  - Platform autonomy perception
  - Cultural appropriateness rating

**Follow-Up (Months 7-12):**
- Monthly assessments (minimal platform access)
- Tracking maintenance of gains
- Measuring independence from platform

**Qualitative Data:**
- Monthly optional interviews (subsample of 50)
- Exit interviews
- Community feedback sessions

### 3.5 Primary Outcomes

**Co-Primary Outcomes:**
1. **Real-World Recovery Indicators:**
   - Substance-free days (increasing)
   - Quality of life scores (improving)
   - Social connection strength (improving)

2. **Platform Independence:**
   - Platform usage decreasing over time (desirable)
   - User autonomy maintained
   - No signs of platform addiction

**Success Defined:** Improved real-world outcomes PLUS decreasing platform usage over time

### 3.6 Secondary Outcomes

**Safety and Ethics:**
- Platform addiction indicators (none expected)
- Autonomy perception scores (high maintained)
- Privacy concern incidents (none expected)
- Cultural appropriateness ratings (high)

**Implementation:**
- User satisfaction
- Feature usage patterns
- Technical issues
- Completion rates

**Mental Health:**
- Depression/anxiety scores
- Crisis events and resolution
- Mental health service utilization

### 3.7 Monitoring and Safety

**Weekly Monitoring:**
- Usage pattern review (automated)
- Red flags:
  - >30 min/day average
  - >5 sessions/day
  - Increasing usage over time (concerning trend)
  - Crisis signals

**Monthly Ethics Board Review:**
- Aggregate usage patterns
- Safety concerns
- Ethical issues
- Protocol modifications if needed

**Participant Check-Ins:**
- Monthly phone calls by research assistant
- Assess wellbeing and concerns
- Not assessment, just connection
- Option for counselor referral

**Stopping Rules:**
- Individual: Participant experiencing harm, wishes to withdraw, requires higher level of care
- Study: Ethics board identifies systemic ethical violation, platform causing net harm

## 4. Risk-Benefit Analysis

### 4.1 Potential Risks

**Low Risk:**
- Platform dependency (mitigated by anti-addiction design and monitoring)
- Privacy breach (mitigated by encryption, POPIA compliance, data minimization)
- Cultural insensitivity (mitigated by cultural adaptation and community governance)
- Time burden (minimal: <30 min/day maximum, assessments brief)

**Medium Risk:**
- Participant expects platform to replace needed treatment (mitigated by education, human-first design)
- Platform not helpful, participant disappointed (mitigated by realistic expectations, alternatives provided)

**Overall Risk Level:** Low to Medium - with extensive safeguards in place

### 4.2 Potential Benefits

**Direct Benefits to Participants:**
- Access to culturally appropriate support resources
- Connection to local human services
- Crisis detection and intervention
- 24/7 supplementary support (AI companion)
- No cost, convenient access

**Societal Benefits:**
- Advance knowledge of ethical AI in healthcare
- Model for vulnerable population protection
- Contribution to SA substance abuse support landscape
- Open-source platform for adaptation

**Risk-Benefit Ratio:** Favorable - low risks with extensive safeguards, potential meaningful benefits

## 5. Data Management and Analysis

### 5.1 Data Management

**Storage:**
- Encrypted database on SA servers
- Separate: identifiable data, research data, platform usage data
- Access controls (role-based)
- Regular backups
- Audit trail of access

**Retention:**
- Identifiable data: Destroyed within 90 days of study completion
- Anonymized research data: Retained for 5 years per institutional policy
- Platform usage data: Anonymized and retained for analysis
- Participants can request deletion anytime

**Sharing:**
- No sharing of identifiable data
- Anonymized data shared with ethics board
- Aggregate data for publication
- Open data (anonymized) with participant consent

### 5.2 Statistical Analysis Plan

**Primary Analysis:**
- **Real-world outcomes:** Linear mixed models tracking substance-free days, QOL over time
- **Platform independence:** Usage trajectory analysis (expect decreasing trend)
- **Success definition:** Improved outcomes + decreasing usage

**Secondary Analysis:**
- Safety indicators (descriptive)
- Subgroup analyses (age, province, substance type)
- Cultural appropriateness by group
- Qualitative thematic analysis

**Power Calculation:**
- N=200 provides 80% power to detect medium effect size (d=0.5) in primary outcomes
- Alpha=0.05, two-tailed
- Accounts for 20% attrition
